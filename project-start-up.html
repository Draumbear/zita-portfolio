Project Start-Up

Project 4 — Final Project Bachelor Fashion Technology — Hogent

Welcome to the page of our Project Start-Up, the final project at Hogent Fashion Technology.

For this project, we were asked to optimise an existing, innovative and sustainable product or to develop our own product according to the principles of the circular economy. Team Makovi chose the latter — we designed and developed a pair of trousers for young women with limited mobility.

Welcome to Makovi! We are a brand that designs adaptable fashion for women aged 20-35 with mobile restrictions that is elegant and fashionable. With the first design Grace, a pair of trousers was created that establishes inclusivity and self-expression, with an eye for beauty and simplicity.

Makovi's name comes from the Slavic word for 'poppy'. The poppy is a symbol of resilience, strength and vulnerability — a reference to Makovi's target audience. Inspired by natural beauty and Scandinavian style, timeless simplicity comes to life in the designs. The colour palette — sage green, poppy red, petroleum blue, beige and black — emphasises the aesthetic value of our simple designs with subtle details.

At Makovi, we are all about the customer experience. We design stylish, timeless and comfortable clothing that is accessible to everyone. Inclusiveness is key, as is our commitment to innovation and sustainability. With a lifetime warranty and free repairs, carried out at our Ghent office, we offer not only quality fashion, but also customer satisfaction.

The design: Grace Trousers

Makovi developed its first trousers, model Grace. This is an elegant design that sits high on the waist and has wider pant legs. It is a pair of trousers adaptable to different people with different needs. The needs of the target group were taken into account when choosing fastenings and haberdashery. Trousers were developed that give the wearer independence and freedom to dress with ease and confidence.

The trousers open in the front with a Velcro fastener on both the left and right. The wearer can open these on one or both sides to put the trousers on and take them off — a closure that also makes it easier for anyone who helps the wearer with dressing or undressing. The waistband was adapted to this closure. It was opted not to add pockets at the side seam, as they are not comfortable when sitting. A piped pocket was added on the back panel instead, for both aesthetic and functional purposes. At the bottom of the trouser legs there is an elastic band with a Velcro fastener, which can be tightened to narrow the leg — useful for wheelchair users, and a way for the wearer to customise the trousers to their own style. On the front panel of the left leg, at thigh level, there is an embroidered poppy — a subtle reference to Makovi's brand name and logo. To add extra elegance, there is a stitched-in crease in the front and back panel.

When designing, attention was paid to using raw materials that are easily recyclable or have a lower impact on the environment.

My contribution: The Life Cycle Analysis

For this project, I was responsible for thinking through all the sustainable decisions Makovi makes when designing, producing and retailing the trousers. It was a big challenge, as it was my first time writing a Life Cycle Analysis, but it was very educational.

When designing a garment in the fast fashion world we live in today, there are a lot of conscious decisions to make when you want to contribute as little as possible to landfills. At Makovi, we tried our best to find sustainable materials or alternatives without compromising on quality. For example, our trousers are made entirely out of tencel lyocell, as is the yarn used for the stitching, and the brand and care label. Tencel Lyocell is a very sustainable fibre made by Lenzing, which uses great sustainable systems during production. As almost everything is made out of the same fibre, it is much easier to recycle the garment once it reaches the end of its life.

This is just a short summary of the base layer of our sustainability. In the Life Cycle Analysis you can find all the information about our sustainable choices regarding the haberdashery, how the fibres, fabric and clothing are produced, how we sell, transport and distribute the garment, how the user can take the best care of their trousers to give it a very long life, and how we recycle our garments and which SDGs we apply at our brand.

Obviously, the Life Cycle Analysis isn't the only thing we researched and established for our brand. When designing the trousers, we had to very carefully select all the fabrics, yarns, haberdashery... that would be used. Nel wrote out the whole Sourcing case for us — she describes all the details, how everything was dyed, the certifications... We also cannot produce anything without a technical pack, created by Elodie in collaboration with Joëlle, containing the design sketch and details, the size specs and measurement chart, labelling and packaging, cutting report, bill of labour and bill of materials.

The most important part of the project was the business plan and financial plan. As we developed a start-up, the financial aspect is something we cannot miss. This was the responsibility of Iris and Lara, and they did really well — mapping out demographics, doing a market analysis, and outlining how we're affording to build up and grow the brand.
